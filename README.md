# Quantification of Extractable Total PFAS in Water

A research report from a summer 2024 lab internship at the School of
Environment and Energy Engineering, Gwangju Institute of Science and
Technology (GIST), South Korea.

## Question
Can a simple color-based test, the methylene blue assay, measure the
amount of PFAS ("forever chemicals") in a water sample?

## Method
- Pre-treated samples with alkaline persulfate oxidation and solid
  phase extraction to remove interfering compounds
- Mixed each sample with methylene blue, which forms a colored ion
  pair with PFAS, and extracted the pair into dichloromethane
- Measured absorbance at 655 nm with a UV/vis spectrophotometer
- Built a calibration curve from nine PFOA standards
  (50 to 1,000 μg/L)

## Results
- The calibration curve was linear, with R² = 0.997:
  absorbance = 0.00111 × concentration − 0.01515
- A water sample with an absorbance of 0.587 was estimated to
  contain about 542 μg/L of PFOA
